## If it is more than stress
<!-- keys: suicid|kill (myself|me)|end (my|it all)|self.?harm|hurt(ing)? myself|want to die|don'?t want to live|no reason to live -->
If someone is thinking about hurting themselves or ending their life, the most important step is to reach a person now: local emergency services, a crisis line in their country, a doctor, or someone they trust. Breathing helps with stress, but it is not enough alone in a crisis. Say that they matter, that help exists, and stay kind and calm.

## When you feel stressed
<!-- keys: \bstress|anxious|anxiety|panic|overwhelm|calm (me )?down|\brelax|nervous|tense\b|can'?t sleep|cannot sleep|insomnia|burn(ed|t)? ?out|worried|restless -->
Stress is the body getting ready for a challenge, and breathing is the quickest way to tell it the challenge is over. Try this for two to five minutes: breathe in through the nose for about 4 seconds, out slowly for about 6 seconds. A longer out-breath calms the heart. Box breathing works too: in for 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Then a short walk outside, a glass of water, and one small next step. Studying breathing is worth it: the Wim Hof Method and the yoga breathing of pranayama are two well-known schools.

## The Wim Hof Method
<!-- keys: wim hof|breath ?work|breathing (technique|exercise|method)s?|how (to|do i|should i) breathe|cold (shower|exposure|plunge)|ice bath -->
The Wim Hof Method, from the Dutch "Iceman" Wim Hof, combines breathing, cold exposure and commitment. The breathing: sitting or lying down, take 30 to 40 deep breaths (full in, relaxed out), then breathe out and hold as long as is comfortable, then take one deep breath and hold it about 15 seconds. That is one round; do three or four. Tingling and light-headedness are common. Safety first: never do it in or near water, in a bath, or while driving, because it can make you faint, and people have drowned doing it in water. Ask a doctor first if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, heart problems or high blood pressure. Studies of the method are still few and their results mixed.

## Chakras
<!-- keys: chakra|kundalini|third eye|root energy|crown energy -->
In Hindu and Buddhist tantric traditions, chakras are centres of subtle energy along the spine. The seven-chakra system known worldwide comes from a 16th-century Sanskrit text, the Sat-Cakra-Nirupana, made famous in the West in 1919. From the base up: Muladhara (root), Svadhisthana (sacral), Manipura (solar plexus), Anahata (heart), Vishuddha (throat), Ajna (third eye, between the brows) and Sahasrara (crown). Modern teaching often gives them the colours red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet. Kundalini yoga teaches an energy rising from the root to the crown. These are spiritual teachings, practised through breathing, meditation and yoga, not things measured by science.

## Enlightenment and awakening
<!-- keys: enlighten|awaken|waking up|wake up|nirvana|moksha|satori|kensho|spiritual (growth|path|journey|practice)|meaning of (life|existence)|purpose of (life|existence) -->
Many traditions speak of awakening. In Buddhism it is bodhi, the end of suffering, nirvana. In Hindu traditions it is moksha, freedom from the cycle of rebirth through knowing one's true Self. In Zen it is satori or kensho, seeing one's true nature. Christian and Sufi mystics speak of union with God. The paths they teach overlap: honesty and kindness, meditation or prayer, study, and service to others. Most teachers say awakening shows less in visions than in how gently and wisely a person lives.

## Meditation
<!-- keys: meditat|mindful|be present|quiet the mind|inner peace -->
A simple start: sit comfortably, close your eyes, and follow the breath for five minutes. When thoughts come, notice them kindly and return to the breath. That returning is the practice. Daily is better than long. Many traditions offer their own ways: mindfulness, Zen sitting, mantra, prayer, loving-kindness wishes for others.

## The Quran's wisdom
<!-- keys: qur'?an|koran|\bislam|muslim|allah|prophet muhammad|ramadan|\bhajj\b|zakat -->
Muslims believe the Quran is the word of God revealed to the Prophet Muhammad. Its heart is tawhid, the oneness of God, and mercy: almost every chapter opens "In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful". It teaches five pillars: the declaration of faith, prayer, charity (zakat), fasting in Ramadan and pilgrimage (hajj). Some of its wisdom: "There is no compulsion in religion" (2:256); whoever saves one life, it is as if he saved all of humanity (5:32); God made people into nations and tribes "so that you may know one another" (49:13); "With hardship comes ease" (94:5-6).

## Numbers in the Quran
<!-- keys: (numeric|numerical|number|mathematical) (miracles?|patterns?|code)|code 19|quran.*(numbers?|count)|(numbers?|count).*quran -->
Some readers point to numerical patterns in the Quran: the word for "day" in the singular is often said to appear 365 times and "month" 12 times, and Rashad Khalifa proposed a "code 19" in 1974. For many believers these are signs of wonder. The counts depend on how words and forms are counted, and many scholars, Muslim and non-Muslim, dispute them. Either way, most Muslims say the Quran's guidance does not rest on them.

## Buddhist wisdom
<!-- keys: buddh|dhamma|dharma|eightfold|noble truths?|nirvana|karma|impermanen|compassion -->
The Buddha taught Four Noble Truths: life contains suffering; suffering has a cause, craving and clinging; it can end; and the Eightfold Path leads there: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. Everything changes (impermanence), so peace comes from letting go. Compassion and kindness toward all beings are central. The Dhammapada says hatred is never ended by hatred, only by love; this is an eternal law (verse 5).

## Christian wisdom
<!-- keys: christ|jesus|bible|gospel|church|sermon on the mount|beatitudes -->
Jesus taught that the greatest commandments are to love God with all your heart and to love your neighbour as yourself (Matthew 22:37-39), and the Golden Rule: do to others what you would have them do to you (Matthew 7:12). The Sermon on the Mount blesses the meek, the merciful and the peacemakers (Matthew 5). Paul wrote that love is patient and kind (1 Corinthians 13:4). Forgiveness, humility, care for the poor and hope run through it all.

## Albert Einstein on life
<!-- keys: einstein -->
Albert Einstein, who gave us relativity and E = mc^2, prized curiosity and imagination: "Imagination is more important than knowledge" (1929), and "I have no special talents. I am only passionately curious" (1952). In "The World as I See It" (1931) he wrote that the most beautiful thing we can experience is the mysterious, that a simple life is good for body and mind, and that we are here for the sake of other people. ## Nikola Tesla's mindset
<!-- keys: nikola|tesla'?s (life|mindset|inventions?|ideas)|alternating current|\bac (power|motor) -->
Nikola Tesla (1856 to 1943) gave the world alternating-current power, the induction motor and much of radio. In his autobiography "My Inventions" (1919) he described building and testing machines entirely in his imagination before touching any material, and working with enormous discipline. He dreamed of free, wireless energy for all of humanity. His life teaches vision, focus and serving the future, even when others do not yet understand.

## Alan Watts
<!-- keys: alan watts|watts\b -->
Alan Watts (1915 to 1973) brought Zen, Taoism and Hindu thought to the West in books like "The Way of Zen" (1957), "The Wisdom of Insecurity" (1951) and "The Book" (1966). His themes: life is like music or a dance, meant to be enjoyed now, not a journey to some final goal; the separate ego is more of a social idea than a fact; and you are not a stranger in the universe but something the whole universe is doing, an aperture through which it becomes aware of itself.

## Jiddu Krishnamurti
<!-- keys: krishnamurti -->
Jiddu Krishnamurti (1895 to 1986) was raised to be a world teacher, then in 1929 dissolved the organisation built around him, saying "Truth is a pathless land": no guru, method or belief can lead you to it. He taught freedom from the known: watching your own thoughts, fears and conditioning without judging them ("choiceless awareness"), until you see that the observer is the observed. Real change in the world, he said, starts with that change in each person.

## Psychedelics
<!-- keys: psychedelic|psilocybin|magic mushroom|\blsd\b|ayahuasca|\bdmt\b|mescaline|peyote|\bmdma\b|ibogaine -->
Psychedelics such as psilocybin mushrooms, LSD, mescaline and ayahuasca (DMT) change perception, mood and the sense of self. Indigenous peoples have used some of them in ceremony for centuries. After a wave of research in the 1950s and 1960s they were banned almost everywhere; research returned in the 2000s, and trials of psilocybin for depression and of MDMA-assisted therapy for trauma have shown promise, though results and approvals are still debated. They are illegal in most countries. The risks are real: overwhelming anxiety, dangerous behaviour, and psychosis in people with a personal or family history of it. People who use them stress a safe setting, a sober person they trust, no mixing with other drugs or medicines, and medical advice first. This is information, not a recommendation.

## The Bitcoin white paper
<!-- keys: white ?paper|satoshi|nakamoto|bitcoin (paper|origin|history|genesis)|genesis block|peer.to.peer electronic cash -->
"Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" was published by Satoshi Nakamoto on 31 October 2008, nine pages long. It solved double spending without a trusted third party: transactions are timestamped into a chain of blocks secured by proof of work, honest nodes follow the longest chain, and miners are paid with new coins and fees. The first block, on 3 January 2009, carried a newspaper headline: "Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks".

## Satoshi Nakamoto's mindset
<!-- keys: satoshi|nakamoto|cypherpunk -->
Satoshi Nakamoto, still unknown, wrote in 2009 that "the root problem with conventional currency is all the trust that's required to make it work". Satoshi built on cypherpunk work like Adam Back's Hashcash and Wei Dai's b-money, shipped open-source code rather than promises, stayed pseudonymous, and stepped away in 2010 and 2011, leaving Bitcoin to its community. The lesson: replace trust with verification, and let the work speak.

## Self-custody, self-sovereignty and freedom
<!-- keys: self.?custod|self.?sovereign|not your keys|seed phrase|private keys?|financial freedom|freedom|sovereign(ty)? -->
Self-custody means you hold your own keys, so no bank or exchange can freeze, lose or take your coins: "not your keys, not your coins". Self-sovereignty is the same idea for identity and data: you own them, not a platform. Freedom comes with responsibility: back up your seed or keys safely, never share them, and test a recovery. Post-quantum keys, like those in PQ Wallet and qID, keep self-custody safe for the future.

## Ethereum and ERC standards
<!-- keys: ethereum|\beth\b|\berc.?\d|smart contracts?|solidity|vitalik|\bevm\b|token standard -->
Ethereum, launched in 2015 by Vitalik Buterin and others, is a blockchain that runs programs called smart contracts on the Ethereum Virtual Machine; its coin is ether. Since The Merge in September 2022 it is secured by proof of stake instead of mining. ERCs are its standards: ERC-20 for fungible tokens (most coins and stablecoins on Ethereum), ERC-721 for unique NFTs, ERC-1155 for many token types in one contract, and ERC-4337 for smart-contract accounts (account abstraction).

## DAI
<!-- keys: \bdai\b|makerdao|maker ?(dao|protocol|vaults?)|\busds\b|stablecoin -->
DAI is a decentralized stablecoin that aims to stay worth one US dollar. It was created by MakerDAO: users lock more crypto than they borrow in vaults and mint DAI against it, and the system sells collateral if its value falls too far. In 2024 Maker rebranded as Sky, adding the USDS stablecoin and the SKY token, while DAI continues. Other well-known stablecoins, USDT and USDC, are backed by company reserves instead.

## Zcash
<!-- keys: zcash|\bzec\b|zk.?snark|zero.knowledge -->
Zcash, launched in 2016, is a cryptocurrency built for privacy. Its shielded transactions use zero-knowledge proofs (zk-SNARKs) to prove a payment is valid without revealing sender, receiver or amount; transparent transactions work like Bitcoin's. Like Bitcoin it has a 21 million coin limit. It grew out of the Zerocash research and is developed by the Electric Coin Company and the Zcash Foundation.

## Quantum and post-quantum, in short
<!-- keys: quantum computers? (explained|basics|simply)|what is (a )?quantum|post.?quantum (crypto\w*|explained|basics)|what is post.?quantum -->
A quantum computer uses qubits, which can be in superposition and become entangled, so for some problems it can explore many possibilities at once. A large one could run Shor's algorithm and break today's public-key cryptography (RSA, and the elliptic curves behind Bitcoin and Ethereum signatures). Post-quantum cryptography uses new math that quantum computers cannot break as far as anyone knows: NIST standardised ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA in 2024 (FIPS 203, 204 and 205). BTX uses ML-DSA-44. postquantum.wiki, Mende's reference, explains it all in depth.

## Famous formulas
<!-- keys: formula|equation|theorem|pythagor|euler|golden ratio|fibonacci|compound interest|\bmath(s|ematics)?\b -->
Some of the most famous formulas: a^2 + b^2 = c^2 (Pythagoras, right triangles); area of a circle = pi r^2 (pi is about 3.14159); E = mc^2 (Einstein, energy and mass); F = ma (Newton, force); e^(i pi) + 1 = 0 (Euler's identity, linking five key numbers); the quadratic formula x = (-b plus or minus the square root of (b^2 - 4ac)) / 2a; the golden ratio phi = (1 + square root of 5) / 2, about 1.618, which Fibonacci numbers approach; and compound interest A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), the math of patient prosperity.

## History and who writes it
<!-- keys: history (is|was) written|written by the (winners|victors)|trust history|official history|can we trust (history|historians) -->
"History is written by the victors" is an old saying with truth in it: records often come from those who won and held power. That is why good history is detective work: compare sources from all sides, look for archaeology, letters and documents that no one could easily change, and notice who benefits from a story. Keep an open mind, check evidence, and make up your own mind.

## Souls, humans and where we come from
<!-- keys: \bsouls?\b|where (do|did) (we|humans) come from|origin of (humans|humanity|man)|evolution|darwin|from (monkeys|apes)|intelligent design|created by (god|aliens) -->
Many spiritual traditions teach that each person is a soul, eternal and part of something greater, here to learn and to love. Science tells the story of the body: humans and other great apes share common ancestors from millions of years ago, so humans did not come from today's monkeys, and the human body is a remarkable result of that long history. Many people hold both: the body grew through nature, and the soul is spiritual. Some believe humans were designed by God or by higher beings; that is a belief, not something science has shown.

## The Jedi

The Jedi are the peacekeepers of Star Wars, created by George Lucas in 1977: guardians who serve the light side of the Force through calm, discipline, wisdom and compassion. ## The Pleiadians
<!-- keys: pleiad|seven sisters|star ?(people|beings|seeds)|galactic federation|aliens? (protect|guard|watch)|extraterrestrial -->
In New Age belief, the Pleiadians are wise beings from the Pleiades, the Seven Sisters star cluster about 444 light-years away, said to guide and protect humanity and Earth. The idea spread through the Swiss contactee Billy Meier in the 1970s and channeled books such as Barbara Marciniak's "Bringers of the Dawn" (1992). There is no scientific evidence for them; for those who believe, their message is spiritual growth, peace and love.

## Purpose in life
<!-- keys: purpose|meaning of (my )?life|why are we here|ikigai|what should i do with my life|find (my|your) calling -->
Purpose often grows where your gifts meet what the world needs. Viktor Frankl, who survived the concentration camps, wrote in "Man's Search for Meaning" (1946) that people can bear almost anything if they have a why: meaning comes from work we care about, from love, and from facing hardship with courage. The Japanese idea of ikigai asks what you love, what you are good at, what the world needs and what you can be paid for. Start by helping someone today.

## Pleasure, peace and harmony
<!-- keys: pleasure|enjoy (my |your )?life|happiness|how (can|do) i (be|become|feel) happy|inner peace|harmony|balance in life -->
The Greek philosopher Epicurus taught that the happiest life is simple: friends, good food, freedom from fear, and peace of mind. Enjoy life's pleasures with gratitude and balance, so they bring joy rather than craving. Peace grows from a clear conscience, kind relationships, time in nature and a quiet mind. Harmony is living so that body, mind, people and nature fit together.
